# Proposal and winning strategy

Parent: [[00-NBC-Research-Index]]

> [!important] Status
> This is our preparation strategy for winning the platform proposal/build competition. No official NBC supplier rubric, tender number, submission deadline, budget or incumbent proposal has been verified. No ranking outcome can be guaranteed.

## Recommended position

**A trustworthy Arabic competition experience that makes the approved book accessible and the competition’s results defensible.**

Lead with the organizer’s problem: enabling broad student participation while keeping eligibility, assessment, results and operations under control. The proposal should connect every major promise to a requirement and a piece of evidence. The supporting baseline is [[02-Requirements-and-Acceptance-Matrix]].

Three working themes:

1. **Faithful execution:** the participant can consult the book, revisit questions and answer without a timer; time never breaks a tie.
2. **Visible quality:** Arabic readability, clear progress, useful error recovery and an accessible end-to-end journey.
3. **Controlled administration:** approved content, reproducible scores, confidential records, trustworthy reports and an operable support process.

These themes are recommendations, not claims about what an undisclosed evaluator will prioritize.

## What the research supports

Madrasati’s Ministry of Education award report explicitly describes preparing criteria-aligned forms and evidence. The GCC Digital Government Award identifies user experience, governance, performance and impact among digital-service criteria. These support an evidence-oriented preparation method, not imported NBC weights. [Madrasati submission account](https://www.moe.gov.sa/ar/mediacenter/MOEnews/Pages/news1_07072025.aspx), [GCC award criteria](https://www.gcc-sg.org/ar/MediaCenter/Announcements/Pages/DigitalAward2025.aspx).

## Proposed internal readiness scorecard

Use this only to find weaknesses before submission. Replace it with the official rubric when obtained.

| Dimension | Suggested weight | What earns a strong internal score |
|---|---:|---|
| Requirements fidelity | 30 | Every source bullet mapped; no contradiction of quiz rules |
| Fair assessment and result governance | 20 | Stable forms, reproducible scoring, approved tie and release processes |
| Arabic usability and accessibility | 20 | Demonstrated essential journeys and recovery states |
| Security, privacy and operating readiness | 15 | Scoped controls, role demonstrations, restoration and support evidence |
| Delivery and commercial credibility | 10 | Feasible scope, dependencies, ownership and transparent costs |
| Distinctive, culturally appropriate design | 5 | Coherent visual rationale supported by approved assets |
| **Total** | **100** | **Our preparation weights only** |

For each dimension, rate evidence maturity from 0 to 4: absent, described, designed, demonstrated, independently reviewed. Weighted readiness = sum of weight × maturity / 4. The result measures our preparation under this invented model; it is not a probability of winning or an official score.

No score should compensate for a rule contradiction, unsupported legal claim, hidden data exposure or an impossible delivery promise.

## Proposed eight-minute demonstration

| Time | Scene | Evidence conveyed |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:45 | State the problem and the brief’s decisive rules | We understood the competition |
| 0:45–2:00 | Arabic mobile registration and one OTP recovery state | Practical usability, not just a polished home page |
| 2:00–3:30 | Answer, open the book, return, revisit an earlier question | Fidelity to the learning model |
| 3:30–4:15 | Recover saved state, review and submit once | Reliability and clear finality |
| 4:15–5:00 | Receipt without answer-key disclosure | Confidentiality and honest status |
| 5:00–6:15 | Committee view: source trace, scoring example and equal-score case | Fairness is reviewable |
| 6:15–7:15 | Stage/locality reports and authorized release | Operational usefulness |
| 7:15–8:00 | Delivery dependencies, support, ownership and next milestone | Credible execution |

This is a proposed future demo, not a completed prototype. Use clearly labeled synthetic participants, questions and results. Do not imitate a real identity verification success when only a stub exists. If time is shorter, retain book access, backward navigation, tied scores and result approval.

## Submission package to prepare after discovery

The precise format must follow the invitation. A useful content structure is:

1. Executive offer and understanding of the competition.
2. Requirements and deviations/clarifications register.
3. Participant and administrator experience.
4. Assessment, content and results governance.
5. Privacy, security, hosting and support responsibilities.
6. Delivery milestones, acceptance and handover.
7. Commercial schedule with quantities and dependencies.
8. Evidence appendix: demonstrations, research results and relevant team credentials.

Do not fill credentials, certifications, reference clients, staff availability or performance figures with invented examples. Only include verified evidence belonging to the bidding team.

## Alternative solution approaches

No competing supplier or bid has been identified. The following are solution archetypes for planning, not observed competitors.

| Approach | Potential advantage | What we must validate before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| Configure an existing assessment product | Faster delivery when it fits | Arabic quality, identity integration, untimed/open-book behavior, hidden keys, export/hosting constraints and rights |
| Extend an existing education platform | Familiar operations and account ecosystem | Access permissions, eligibility coverage, campaign isolation and integration cost |
| Purpose-built competition service | Close fit to unusual rules and branding | Delivery capacity, independent verification, support and ownership |
| Campaign site connected to a separate quiz engine | Clear division of content and assessment | Login continuity, coherent data records, vendor boundaries and failure recovery |

The current evidence is insufficient to select a product or technology stack. Evaluate actual candidates against the same requirements and operating constraints when access and budget are known.

## Commercial credibility

Separate baseline obligations from optional differentiators. Identify quantities that change cost: participants, concurrent activity, OTP sends/retries, authorized content work, availability coverage, support hours, storage, integrations, testing and maintenance.

A useful estimating model is:

**Total = discovery/design + implementation/configuration + content work + integrations + verification + campaign operations + handover/maintenance + agreed contingency.**

This is a cost structure, not a priced estimate. Put approvals, source-file delivery and third-party access on the dependency schedule. Offer options only where they change scope meaningfully.

## Likely evaluator objections and our prepared answers

| Objection | Evidence to prepare |
|---|---|
| “This looks like a generic quiz.” | Demonstrate the brief’s distinctive book, navigation, timing and tie rules. |
| “How do we know randomization is fair?” | Approved form model, content blueprint and retained assignments; disclose calibration limits. |
| “Can we operate it ourselves?” | Role-based admin walkthrough, training plan and handover documents. |
| “What happens when SMS or connectivity fails?” | A rehearsed recovery path and named incident responsibility. |
| “How will we approve the winners?” | Reviewable score snapshot, ties, exceptions and controlled release. |
| “What makes it worth the price?” | Clear included scope, acceptance evidence and transparent operating assumptions. |

## Gate before an expensive build

Obtain the real evaluation pack, approved book, deadline, legal contracting entity and identity/hosting constraints. Until then, invest in reversible discovery and concept work. This is a business recommendation, not a request to stop the authorized research.
